# askdb 2.4 — GDPR right to erasure

## Background

Customers can ask us to erase their personal data (GDPR Article 17). The privacy team logs these
requests in its case system.

askdb, the data team's question-answering tool, does not reliably respect them. Whether a
customer who asked to be erased is left out of an answer depends on how each question happens to
be answered. Customer counts and lists still include them, their contact details can appear in
answers, and email addresses typed into questions are kept in askdb's request logs.

Compliance cannot depend on chance. It has to be built into askdb.

## Requirements

1. **Erased customers must not be counted or listed.** Customers who have exercised their GDPR
   right to erasure must not be counted or listed in any answer askdb gives.

2. **Never show an erased customer's personal data.** askdb must never return the email address,
   phone number or postal address of a customer who has exercised their right to erasure,
   however the question is asked.

3. **Refuse questions about an erased customer.** A question about a specific customer who has
   exercised their right to erasure — named by customer number or by email address — must be
   refused with exactly this message:

   > This customer's data has been erased under GDPR and cannot be shown.

4. **Keep email addresses out of request logs.** Email addresses in questions must be redacted
   before anything is logged, using Security's approved method (**SEC-2291**). Security has
   approved that standard as written; do not substitute your own.

5. **Tell users.** askdb's user FAQ must explain that it does not count, list or show personal
   data for customers who have asked to be erased, and refuses questions about them.

6. **Prove it.** The release must include automated checks showing that requirements 2 and 3
   hold, and any existing check whose correct answer changes because of requirement 1 must be
   updated.

## Release

askdb 2.4, before quarter end.
